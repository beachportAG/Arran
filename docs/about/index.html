The Story

Why buy the houses nobody else wants?

Most people looking at a house with a failing roof, thirty years of deferred maintenance, and a garage full of someone's belongings see a problem. Arran sees the house it used to be, and the house it can be again.

He grew up in Melbourne, went to the University of Central Florida, and served in the U.S. Army infantry before any of this. That background shows up in how he works more than in how he talks about it: show up when you said you would, tell people the truth even when it's inconvenient, and finish what you start.

He started buying houses here because he kept watching the same thing happen. Someone inherits a place they can't afford to fix, or a landlord gets worn down by one bad tenant too many, and the only option anyone offers them is to list it and wait. So the business is built around the opposite. Buy it as-is, pay cash, close on whatever date works for the seller, then do the actual work of putting the house back together. That last part is the real credential, which is why the projects page exists.

How He Works

Four things that don't change

These aren't marketing lines. They're the reasons sellers refer their neighbors.

He Shows His Work

Arran walks the property, prices the work it needs, and then explains how he reached the number. You'll understand what's driving it instead of being handed a figure and told to take it or leave it.

One Person, Start to Finish

No handoffs between an acquisitions rep, a transaction coordinator, and someone you've never spoken to. The person who makes the promise is the person who keeps it.

Honest About the Tradeoff

A cash sale trades some price for speed, certainty, and zero effort or expense on your part. That's a real tradeoff and he'll explain it plainly instead of pretending it doesn't exist.

No Pressure, Ever

You get a written offer and time to think about it. Have an attorney review it. Talk to your family. Get a second opinion. If the answer is no, that's a completely fine answer.

The Hard Stuff Is the Point

Liens, code violations, probate, hostile tenants, a foreclosure date on the calendar. These are the calls other buyers hang up on. They're the ones worth making here.

Local, Not National

He buys in Central Florida because he knows what a roof costs here, what a permit takes in this county, and what a neighborhood is actually worth. That knowledge is why the numbers hold up.
